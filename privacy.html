Privacy Policy

Drills & Pieces: The Hobby & Craft Timer

Effective date: November 18, 2026

The short version

Your data stays on your phoneNo accounts or sign-inFree, with ads by Google AdMobOptional one-time purchase removes adsNo analytics or tracking of our own

Drills & Pieces is a hobby tracker for diamond art, puzzles and other craft projects. I don’t collect your information, and the app doesn’t send it to me or to any server. The one thing that goes online is the ads, explained below under Ads.

What the app stores

Everything you enter stays on your device, in the app’s private storage:

  • Project details, such as title, type, status, dates, cost, notes, purchase link, theme, rating and drill colors
  • Work-session times from the timer
  • Your drill stock
  • Photos you add or take with your camera, which are copied into the app so they keep working even if you move or delete the original
  • Your settings, such as theme, palette and backup options

The app has no accounts, no sign-in, no analytics of its own, no tracking of its own and no crash-reporting tools. The only outside service is Google AdMob, which shows the ads (see Ads below).

Photos

When you add a photo to a project, you can choose one with Android’s own photo picker, or take a new one with your camera. The app asks for camera permission only when you choose to take a photo, and you can say no and still pick photos from your gallery. The app only gets the photos you pick or take, and it doesn’t use the camera for anything else. Photos you take are copied into the app and stay on your phone.

Backups you create

  • Export and import: when you export a backup, the app saves a file (JSON, or ZIP with photos) to a place you choose. When you import, the app reads only the file you pick.
  • Automatic export (off unless you turn it on): if you turn it on, the app saves backup files on the schedule you choose to a folder you pick, and keeps the number of recent backups you set. You can turn it off at any time in Settings.
  • Wherever you save a backup (your phone, an SD card or a cloud storage app), that place’s own privacy practices apply.

Android’s built-in backup

Android may include the app’s data in your phone’s own Google backup, so it can come back if you move to a new phone. To keep that backup small, photos are left out of cloud backup; projects, sessions, drill stock and settings may be included. That backup is handled by Google under Google’s privacy policy, and you can turn it off in your phone’s settings. When you transfer data directly from an old phone to a new one, photos are included.

Notifications

The app asks permission to show notifications. It uses them only to tell you if an automatic backup export fails. You can say no, or turn notifications off later, and the app still works.

Sharing and copying

If you tap Share, Text project or Copy, the app passes the text (and the cover photo, if you include it) to the app you choose, or puts it on your clipboard. Nothing is shared unless you do this yourself.

Home screen widget

If you add the widget, it shows the selected project’s title, time and cover photo on your home screen, so anyone who can see your screen can see them. The widget’s start/stop button works the same as the timer inside the app.

Links to other sites

The app has links that open in your web browser: purchase links you add, this privacy policy and Discord. Those sites have their own privacy practices.

Ads

The free app shows a small banner ad from Google AdMob. To choose, show and measure ads, and to prevent fraud, Google’s Mobile Ads SDK in the app collects and shares with Google:

  • Your device’s advertising ID and other device identifiers
  • Approximate location, worked out from your IP address
  • Information about your device and the app, such as the model, Android version and app version
  • How you interact with ads, such as views and taps, plus diagnostic information

This information goes to Google, not to me, and Google handles it under its own privacy policy. You can read how Google uses information from apps that show its ads at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Your choices:

  • In the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and other places where the law requires it, the app shows Google’s consent message before any ad loads, so you can choose whether ads may be personalised.
  • You can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your phone’s settings (Settings > Privacy > Ads, or Google > Ads, depending on the phone).

The app does not tell Google anything you enter into it.

Remove ads purchase

Settings > About offers Remove ads, a one-time purchase through Google Play. Google Play handles the whole payment under its own terms and privacy policy; the app and I never see your card or payment details.

The app only learns from Google Play whether your Google account owns Remove ads, and remembers that answer on your phone so ads stay off when you’re offline. It checks again with Google Play when the app opens, so the purchase comes back after a reinstall or on a new phone signed in to the same account. Once you own it, the app stops loading ads and the ad consent message, so Google’s ad service no longer collects the ad information described above.

Google Play

The app is installed through Google Play. Google may collect information under its own privacy policy. I don’t receive that information in a form that identifies you.

Deleting your data

You can delete projects, photos, sessions and drill colors inside the app at any time. Uninstalling the app deletes all of its data from your phone. Backup files you exported stay where you saved them until you delete them.

Children

The app isn’t directed to children under 13, and I don’t knowingly collect information from anyone.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, I’ll update this page and its effective date. The same policy is also published at sites.google.com/view/drillspiecesapp.

Feedback and email

The app has a Feedback option in Settings & Options. When you send feedback, the app opens your own email app with the message filled in, and you choose whether to send it. The app itself doesn’t send anything.

If you send it, I receive your email address, your message, and, only if you leave “Include app and phone details” on, the app version, your Android version and your phone model. Your projects, photos, notes and drill stock are never included.

You can also choose Send anonymously. That opens my “App Feedback” Google Form in your web browser, with your message, the app name and feedback type filled in, plus the app version and phone model only if that switch is on. You review it and tap Submit yourself. The form doesn’t ask for your name, and your email is optional; leave it blank and I can’t tell who sent it. Google hosts the form under its own privacy policy.

I use feedback only to reply to you and to improve the app. I never sell or share it, unless required by law.
