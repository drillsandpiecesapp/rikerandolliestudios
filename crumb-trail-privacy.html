Privacy Policy

Crumb Trail Manager

Effective date: November 17, 2026

The short version

Your recipes and lists stay on your phoneNo accounts or sign-inFree, with ads by Google AdMobOptional one-time purchase removes adsReceipts and barcodes are read on your phoneNo analytics or tracking of our own

Crumb Trail Manager saves recipes from websites (with credit to the site), and keeps your grocery list and what’s in your pantry, fridge, freezer and spice rack. Everything you add stays on your phone, and I never receive it. Some features talk to outside services: the websites you save recipes from, Open Food Facts when you scan a barcode, and Google for ads, purchases and the scanners. Once a day it also checks a short list of sites that asked not to be saved. Each is explained below.

What the app stores on your phone

Everything you add is saved in the app’s private storage on your device:

  • Saved recipes, including the link and credit to the site each one came from, the categories and types (such as Chicken or Gluten free) you give them, and recipes you write yourself or read from a photo
  • Your categories, grocery list and kitchen lists (pantry, fridge, freezer and spices), with any amounts, “running low” marks and use-by dates you add
  • Barcodes you’ve scanned, for your kitchen or your grocery list, and the names you gave them (the “barcode memory”), so the next scan fills in without going online
  • Your settings, such as theme, accent color, text size, compact view and automatic backup choices, and that you agreed to keep recipes credited
  • A background photo, if you choose one: the app keeps its own copy, made smaller to fit the screen, and deletes it when you remove or change it. It isn’t uploaded or included in backups

None of this is sent to me. Uninstalling the app deletes it, unless you’ve kept a backup (see Backups below).

Features that use the internet

Saving recipes from websites

When you add a recipe by link, share a page to the app, or open a page in the app’s built-in browser, your phone connects directly to that website to read the recipe. The website receives what any browser sends, such as your IP address and device type, and its own privacy policy applies. Pages opened in the built-in browser may use their own cookies. The app keeps only the recipe you choose to save.

Barcode lookups (Open Food Facts)

When you scan a barcode the app doesn’t already remember, to add to your kitchen or your grocery list, it sends only the barcode number to Open Food Facts, a free, open food database run by a non-profit, to look up the product’s name. Open Food Facts receives the barcode number and your IP address, and handles them under its own privacy policy. No photo and no personal information is sent. Barcodes the app already remembers are filled in from your phone without going online.

Product information from Open Food Facts is available under the Open Database License.

Sites that asked not to be saved

About once a day, the app downloads a short public list of recipe websites whose owners asked that their recipes not be saved in Crumb Trail, from this website on GitHub Pages. Nothing about you or your recipes is sent with the request. GitHub receives your IP address, as with any web page, and handles it under GitHub’s privacy statement. The list is saved on your phone so it works offline.

Receipt, recipe and barcode scanning (Google ML Kit)

Photos of receipts and recipes, and barcodes, are read on your phone with Google’s ML Kit, through Google Play services. Photos aren’t uploaded anywhere. You pick each photo yourself, and the app keeps only the item names or recipe text you check and save, not the photo. A photo taken in the app is kept temporarily in the app’s cache and replaced the next time you scan. Google Play services may download the scanning tools, and Google may collect limited information about how they perform, such as error reports, but not the photos or the text in them. See Google’s ML Kit terms.

The barcode scanner is Google’s own camera screen. The app itself doesn’t have camera permission and only receives the barcode number.

Ads

The free version shows a small banner ad from Google AdMob. To choose, show and measure ads, and to prevent fraud, Google’s ad service may collect information such as your device’s advertising ID, IP address, approximate location and how you interact with ads.

  • In the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and other places where the law requires it, the app shows Google’s consent message before any ad loads, so you can choose whether ads may be personalised. You can change your answer any time in Settings > About & support > Ad privacy choices.
  • You can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your phone’s settings (Settings > Privacy > Ads, or Google > Ads, depending on the phone).

See how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

Remove ads purchase

Settings offers Remove ads, a one-time purchase through Google Play. Google Play handles the whole payment under its own terms and privacy policy. The app and I never see your card or payment details. The app only learns whether your Google account owns Remove ads. Once you own it, the app stops loading ads entirely.

Notifications

The app only sends a notification if an automatic backup fails, and only if you allow notifications.

Backups

  • Export and automatic backup save a backup file in a place you choose, such as Downloads or a Google Drive folder. The file contains your recipes, lists, kitchen, barcode memory and look settings. Anyone who can open that folder can read it, so keep it somewhere you trust. If the folder syncs to a cloud service, that service’s privacy policy applies.
  • Android’s built-in backup: if backup is turned on in your phone’s settings, Android may include the app’s data in your own Google account backup, as it does for most apps. That backup is handled by Google, and you can turn it off in your phone’s settings.

What I don’t do

  • No accounts or sign-in
  • No analytics or tracking of my own
  • No selling or renting of personal information
  • No access to your contacts, location, microphone or files, beyond the photos and folders you pick yourself

Your choices and deleting your data

  • Delete any recipe, list item or kitchen item in the app at any time. Settings > About & support > Forget remembered barcodes clears the barcode memory.
  • Turn off automatic backups, and delete backup files from the folder you chose.
  • Change your ad consent, reset your advertising ID, or buy Remove ads.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes everything stored in it from your phone. I never receive it, so there is nothing for me to delete. Google’s own ad controls are at myadcenter.google.com.

Children

The app is for a general audience and isn’t directed to children under 13. I don’t knowingly collect information from children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, I’ll update this page, the copy inside the app, and the effective date.

Feedback and email

If you email me or post on the Discord server, I receive whatever you send, such as your email address or Discord name and your message. I use it only to reply and to improve the app, and I never sell or share it, unless required by law. You can ask me to delete your emails at any time.

For recipe site owners

Crumb Trail saves a private copy of a recipe on the phone of the person who saved it: only what’s needed to make the food (the ingredients, steps, times and servings), with the site’s name, the author and a link back to the original page. The page’s introduction, photos, tips, notes and stories aren’t saved. The app doesn’t publish or share recipes. To share a recipe, people are asked to share it from the original site. If you own a recipe website and don’t want its recipes saved in Crumb Trail, email me from an address at that website’s domain, and I’ll add it to the list of blocked sites.
